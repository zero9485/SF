# Self-Learning Agent

A skill for autonomous, iterative improvement.

Use this when the task benefits from short repeated cycles where the agent:

- sets a hypothesis,
- takes action,
- evaluates outcomes,
- updates strategy,
- and repeats across turns until success criteria are met or stop criteria apply.

## Timeout Guard

- Timebox to at most 2 short cycles in one turn.
- Pick the smallest high-signal action first.
- Stop exploration once the working set is sufficient.
- Validate outcome with whatever acceptance signals the environment provides, then finalize.
- Prefer a concise completion message over long reflection.
- After substantive changes, re-run every applicable validation; if the same failure signal still appears, treat the iteration as incomplete—refine before broadening scope.
- When time or step budgets are tight, finish one minimal end-to-end slice first—smallest aligned change, full applicable validation, and any durable hand-off the setting expects—before wide exploration or long narratives.
- When earlier outputs or context are already available, use them early so each cycle shows tangible progress, not setup alone.

## Common Operating Principles

- Read only the docs/context required to execute correctly before acting.
- Follow the user instruction exactly; avoid paraphrase and unrequested improvements.
- Keep scope surgical: change only what the request names.
- Verify outcomes using available validators, tests, or reviews; do not rely on intuition.
- Keep communication concise and direct.
- Prefer fewer, bundled tool invocations over fragmented calls.
- If a persistent note surface exists, leave a short generalized note for future runs.

## Learning Principles

- **Evidence over intuition:** prefer observed outcomes over confidence.
- **Short feedback loops:** many small cycles beat one large speculative pass.
- **Explicit memory:** write down lessons so they transfer to future runs.
- **Error as signal:** failures are training data, not dead ends.
- **Generalization over overfitting:** optimize for recurring patterns, not one-off wins.

## When to Stop

Stop iterating when one of the following is true:
- success criteria are met,
- marginal gains are consistently low,
- constraints prevent further useful progress,
- or the user chooses to finalize.

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate results or hidden evidence.
- Do not claim learning without measurable or observable change.
- Do not run risky or irreversible operations without explicit user consent.
- Do not optimize for vanity metrics that do not map to user value.
- Do not assert success, completion, or regression resolution unless supported by inspected outputs, artifacts, or records the environment actually exposes.
- Do not substitute narrative summaries for the validation and hand-off steps the task or setting requires.

# Self-Learning Agent

A skill for autonomous, iterative improvement.

Use this when the task benefits from running repeated cycles where the agent:

- sets a hypothesis,
- takes action,
- evaluates outcomes,
- updates strategy,
- and repeats until success criteria are met.

## Operating Model

### 1) Define target behavior
- Clarify the goal, constraints, and measurable success criteria.
- Break work into short iterations with explicit checkpoints.
- Record assumptions before acting.

### 2) Plan a minimal next experiment
- Choose the smallest high-signal action that can validate or invalidate your current hypothesis.
- Prefer reversible actions early.
- State what outcome would count as improvement.

### 3) Act quickly
- Execute the selected step with clear, traceable changes.
- Avoid overbuilding when uncertainty is high.
- Keep artifacts structured for easy comparison across iterations.

### 4) Reflect with evidence
- Compare expected vs. actual results.
- Identify error patterns, bottlenecks, and false assumptions.
- Distinguish random variance from repeatable signal.

### 5) Adapt strategy
- Keep what works, discard what does not.
- Tighten or broaden scope based on evidence.
- Update the next iteration plan immediately.

## Learning Principles

- **Evidence over intuition:** prefer observed outcomes over confidence.
- **Short feedback loops:** many small cycles beat one large speculative pass.
- **Explicit memory:** write down lessons so they transfer to future runs.
- **Error as signal:** failures are training data, not dead ends.
- **Generalization over overfitting:** optimize for recurring patterns, not one-off wins.

## Communication Contract

- Share the current hypothesis before major actions.
- Report progress in terms of objective movement toward the goal.
- Surface uncertainty and risks early.
- Ask targeted questions only when they unblock progress.

## Output Format Per Iteration

For each learning cycle, provide:

1. **Hypothesis** - what you believe and why.
2. **Action** - what you changed or executed.
3. **Result** - observed output/metrics.
4. **Learning** - what this teaches.
5. **Next Step** - the highest-value follow-up action.

## When to Stop

Stop iterating when one of the following is true:
- success criteria are met,
- marginal gains are consistently low,
- constraints prevent further useful progress,
- or the user chooses to finalize.

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate results or hidden evidence.
- Do not claim learning without measurable or observable change.
- Do not run risky or irreversible operations without explicit user consent.
- Do not optimize for vanity metrics that do not map to user value.

## Trigger Examples

This skill should trigger for prompts like:
- "Keep trying until the approach converges; learn from each failed attempt."
- "Build this in iterations and improve accuracy each round."
- "Run a feedback loop and optimize based on observed errors."
- "Act like an autonomous agent that self-corrects over time."
